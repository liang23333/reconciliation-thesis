# Comparative Transitional Justice: Truth Commissions and Post-Conflict Governance Timelines

A comparative political and legal research thesis examining the role of Truth and Reconciliation Commissions (TRCs) in post-conflict transitions and the duration required to achieve consolidated, stable governance.

---

## 📊 Executive Summary & Core Metric

Across the five primary global transitional justice case studies analyzed in this thesis—**South Africa**, **Rwanda**, **Chile**, **Sierra Leone**, and **Colombia**—the average duration from the formal cessation of conflict (or authoritarian rule) to the establishment of consolidated stable governance is:

$$\text{Average Transition Duration} = \frac{5 + 9 + 4 + 5 + 6}{5} = \mathbf{5.8 \text{ years}}$$

## 🔍 Methodology & Operational Definitions

To ensure analytical rigor across divergent socio-political contexts, this study standardizes three core transitional benchmarks:

1. **Formal Cessation of Conflict / Authoritarian Rule ($T_0$):**
   - The negotiated settlement, definitive military victory, or peaceful democratic handover terminating active state violence or dictatorial control (e.g., South Africa’s April 1994 elections; Rwanda’s July 1994 RPF victory; Chile’s March 1990 inauguration of Aylwin; Sierra Leone’s January 2002 formal disarmament declaration; Colombia’s November 2016 Final Accord).
2. **Mandate and Operation of Truth Commissions:**
   - Official, temporary, non-judicial or quasi-judicial fact-finding bodies authorized by the state to investigate patterns of gross human rights abuses, foster social healing, and recommend structural institutional reforms.
3. **Consolidated Stable Governance ($T_1$):**
   - Defined in political science and transitional justice literature through institutional consolidation: the adoption and enactment of a permanent post-conflict constitution, successful completion of the formal interim/transitional government phase, and passing Huntington's democratic consolidation benchmark (peaceful transfer or democratic alternation of power without relapse into systemic violence).

## 📂 Repository Structure & Regional Branches

This repository is organized into dedicated Git branches for each regional case study. Each branch contains detailed field notes, primary source documents, commission reports, and legal frameworks specific to that jurisdiction.

### Branch Directory

- [`main`](https://github.com/liang23333/reconciliation-thesis/tree/main): Master repository, cross-case comparative synthesis, quantitative metrics, and thesis bibliography.
- [`case-study/south-africa`](https://github.com/liang23333/reconciliation-thesis/tree/case-study/south-africa): Focus on the South African TRC, CODESA negotiations, the 1996 Constitution, amnesty-for-truth provisions, and the 1999 democratic transition.
- [`case-study/rwanda`](https://github.com/liang23333/reconciliation-thesis/tree/case-study/rwanda): Examination of the National Unity and Reconciliation Commission (NURC), the 2001–2012 *Gacaca* community courts, the 2003 Constitution, and post-genocide state reconstruction.
- [`case-study/chile`](https://github.com/liang23333/reconciliation-thesis/tree/case-study/chile): Analysis of the Rettig Commission (1990–1991), civil-military relations under the 1980 Constitution, the 1994 succession, the Valech Commission (2003–2005), and the 2005 constitutional reforms.
- [`case-study/sierra-leone`](https://github.com/liang23333/reconciliation-thesis/tree/case-study/sierra-leone): Fieldwork and archival notes on the Lomé Accord, the SLTRC (2002–2004), the parallel Special Court for Sierra Leone (SCSL), and the 2007 democratic handover.
- [`case-study/colombia`](https://github.com/liang23333/reconciliation-thesis/tree/case-study/colombia): Investigation into the 2016 Havana Peace Accords, the Comprehensive System (CEV, JEP, UBPD), territorial peace (*paz territorial*), and the 2022 democratic milestone.
