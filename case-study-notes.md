# Case Study: Colombia (South America / Andean Region)

## 1. Historical Context & Conflict Architecture
- **Period of Internal Armed Conflict:** Over 52 years of armed confrontation (1964–2016) involving the State armed forces, Marxist insurgencies (FARC-EP, ELN, EPL, M-19), right-wing paramilitary groups (AUC), and drug trafficking syndicates. The conflict generated more than 9 million registered victims, over 260,000 fatalities (the vast majority civilians), and over 8 million internally displaced persons (IDPs).
- **Formal Cessation of Conflict ($T_0$):** **November 2016** (Signing of the *Final Agreement for the Termination of the Conflict and the Construction of a Stable and Lasting Peace* at the Teatro Colón in Bogotá on November 24, 2016; ratified by Congress on November 30, 2016. The UN Verification Mission certified the complete surrender of arms by ~13,000 FARC-EP fighters in August 2017).

## 2. Truth Commission & The "Comprehensive System"
The 2016 Havana Accords created the world's most structurally sophisticated transitional justice architecture: the *Comprehensive System for Truth, Justice, Reparation and Non-Repetition* (SIVJRNR / *Sistema Integral para la Paz*), composed of three interlocking pillars:
1. **Commission for the Clarification of Truth, Coexistence and Non-Repetition (CEV):**
   - Created by Decree Law 588 of 2017 as an extrajudicial, constitutional, and autonomous body.
   - Chaired by Jesuit priest and philosopher Father Francisco de Roux.
   - Operating period: Formally installed in 2018; operated through June 2022 (extended briefly due to the COVID-19 pandemic).
   - Heard testimony from nearly 30,000 individuals across 28 territorial houses and 24 countries (documenting diaspora and exile experiences).
   - Delivered its comprehensive 10-volume final report *Hay Futuro Si Hay Verdad* ("There is a Future If There is Truth") on **June 28, 2022**.
2. **Special Jurisdiction for Peace (JEP):**
   - Autonomous constitutional court adjudicating macro-cases (e.g., Case 01: Hostage-taking by FARC; Case 03: "False Positives" extrajudicial executions by state security forces). Applies restorative sanctions (*sanciones propias*) prioritizing truth and reparation over punitive imprisonment.
3. **Unit for the Search for Disappeared Persons (UBPD):**
   - Dedicated humanitarian body to search for, locate, and identify over 100,000 disappeared individuals.

## 3. Timeline to Consolidated Stable Governance ($T_1$)
- **Interim Institutional Absorption (2016–2018):** Fast-track legislative ratification, demobilization, and the 2018 election of conservative critic Iván Duque, whose administration tested institutional resilience without abrogating the constitutionalized peace accord.
- **Democratic Alternation Milestone (2022):** The June 2022 presidential elections resulted in the victory and peaceful inauguration of Gustavo Petro—the first leftist president in Colombia’s history and a former insurgent—alongside Afro-Colombian activist Francia Márquez. The demobilized FARC participated peacefully as the *Comunes* political party in Congress, and the new government adopted the CEV Final Report as a foundational national roadmap.
- **Elapsed Duration:** **6 Years** ($2016 \rightarrow 2022$). *(Alternatively: 5 years from disarmament completion in 2017).*

## 4. Key Critical Debates for Thesis Chapters
- **"Territorial Peace" (*Paz Territorial*):** The structural challenge of extending state presence to remote rural peripheries vacated by FARC, where dissident factions, ELN, and Clan del Golfo vie for illicit economies.
- **Restorative vs. Retributive Sanctions in the JEP:** Domestic and international legal evaluations of non-custodial restorative sanctions under Rome Statute standards.
- **Implementation Deficits:** Political resistance, funding bottlenecks, and agrarian land reform delays.
