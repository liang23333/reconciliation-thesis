# Case Study: Sierra Leone (West Africa)

## 1. Historical Context & Conflict Architecture
- **Period of Civil Conflict:** Sierra Leone Civil War (1991–2002), initiated by the Revolutionary United Front (RUF) under Foday Sankoh against successive Freetown governments. Marked by extreme atrocities against civilians, systemic forced child soldier conscription, widespread mutilations/amputations, conflict diamond illicit financing, and mercenary/ECOMOG interventions.
- **Formal Cessation of Conflict ($T_0$):** **January 2002** (On January 18, 2002, President Ahmad Tejan Kabbah declared the 11-year war officially ended following the successful completion of the UNAMSIL disarmament of over 72,000 combatants and destruction of weapons).

## 2. Truth Commission & Hybrid Justice Architecture
Sierra Leone was pioneering in running a dual-track transitional justice mechanism: a truth commission alongside a hybrid criminal court.
1. **Sierra Leone Truth and Reconciliation Commission (SLTRC):**
   - Mandated by the 1999 Lomé Peace Agreement; legally constituted via the *Truth and Reconciliation Commission Act 2000*.
   - Inaugurated in **July 2002**; chaired by Bishop Joseph Christian Humper.
   - Collected nearly 9,000 statements, conducted thematic and regional public hearings throughout 2003, and published its monumental 5-volume report *Witness to Truth* in **October 2004**.
   - Made groundbreaking findings on governance failure, corruption, youth exclusion, and systemic marginalization as root causes of the rebellion.
2. **Special Court for Sierra Leone (SCSL):**
   - Established by bilateral treaty between the UN and the Government of Sierra Leone in January 2002.
   - Targeted those bearing the "greatest responsibility" (indicted leaders of RUF, AFRC, CDF, and Liberian President Charles Taylor).
3. **Institutional Interface / Friction:**
   - Precedent-setting institutional dispute regarding whether the SCSL could subpoena TRC confidential records and testimony, ultimately prompting the TRC to affirm the inviolability of confidential truth-telling.

## 3. Timeline to Consolidated Stable Governance ($T_1$)
- **Immediate Post-War Phase (2002–2005):** Peaceful parliamentary and presidential elections in May 2002 under UNAMSIL protection. Gradual reconstruction of the Sierra Leone Police (SLP) and armed forces. UNAMSIL concluded its mission and withdrew in December 2005.
- **Democratic Alternation Milestone (2007):** In the September 2007 national elections, the ruling Sierra Leone People's Party (SLPP) was defeated by the opposition All People's Congress (APC), and incumbent President Tejan Kabbah peacefully transferred the presidency to opposition leader Ernest Bai Koroma. This peaceful alternation passed the democratic consolidation test without reliance on foreign peacekeepers.
- **Elapsed Duration:** **5 Years** ($2002 \rightarrow 2007$).

## 4. Key Critical Debates for Thesis Chapters
- **Coexistence of Retributive and Restorative Justice:** How the simultaneous presence of the SCSL affected victim and perpetrator willingness to testify before the TRC.
- **Grassroots vs. Formal Mechanisms:** The emergence of localized community-led post-TRC reconciliation initiatives such as *Fambul Tok* ("Family Talk").
- **Structural Root Causes:** Whether post-conflict governance effectively addressed youth unemployment and extractive governance highlighted in the TRC report.
